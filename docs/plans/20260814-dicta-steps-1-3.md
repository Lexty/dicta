# dicta — steps 1–3 of SPEC.md

## Overview

Build dicta from an empty repository (spec only) up to and including step 3 of `SPEC.md`'s acceptance criteria:

- **Step 1** — the whole delivery path with fakes: chord → `dictactl` → daemon → `agtermctl session type`, canned hostile transcript, nothing submitted.
- **Step 2** — the microphone and the models: a signed `.app` that owns the TCC grant, real capture at 16 kHz mono, capture faults, and a Parakeet recogniser warm before the first chord.
- **Step 3** — the text pipeline: the append-only record, reading it back, and the Tier 0 replacement dictionary, with a misfiring rule diagnosable from the record alone.

Out of scope here, deliberately: **step 4** (the external filter — it needs a decision the user wants to make after seeing real recogniser output, D9c) and **step 5** (the sweep that gives every §7 row a test or a checklist entry). Rows of §7 that belong to a component built here are still tested as that component is built; what step 5 adds later is the *audit* that none was missed.

An earlier step-1 skeleton was built, measured, verified end to end and deleted; it survives as commit `3dda6cb`. It is a reference for target layout and for two of the spec's measurements (F4, F5), not something to restore wholesale — the spec has moved on since (`warming` is now a first-class state, the record has a schema, the failure vocabulary is sharper).

### One deliberate departure from the spec's step numbering

§10 files the record under step 3, but step 2's criterion (b) is scored by reading `recognised` out of the record, and invariant 10 ("recognised text always reaches the record before injection") is cheapest to establish while the transcriber is still a fake that returns on demand. The record is therefore built in Task 7, before the microphone. Nothing else moves.

  - Environment (from the skeleton's `CLAUDE.md`, re-verify in Task 1): Swift 6.3.2, `arm64-apple-macosx26.0`, **Command Line Tools only, no full Xcode**.

## Development Approach

- **Testing approach**: **TDD** for everything in `DictaCore` — the state machine, the sanitiser, the record schema and the replacement engine are pure values, and their tests *are* the executable form of §6/§8/§9. **Regular** (code first, then tests) for `DictaRuntime`, where a test drives fakes behind the seams and the fake has to exist first.
- Every seam — `Capture`, `Transcriber`, `Filter`, `Injector`, `Notifier`, `Clock` — has a fake, so the entire lifecycle is drivable with no microphone, no model and no terminal (D19).
- `FakeTranscriber` returns deliberately hostile canned text (a newline, a double space, a trailing space) so a bypassed sanitiser fails loudly at the first end-to-end run, not in production.
- Complete each task fully before moving to the next.
- **CRITICAL: every task MUST include new/updated tests.**
- **CRITICAL: all tests must pass (`bash Scripts/test.sh`) before starting the next task.**
- Hardware, TCC prompts, real speech and real chords cannot be asserted by a test. They are collected in the Post-Completion checklist and never appear as task checkboxes.

## Post-Completion (human verification — §10 and §11, steps 1–3 only)

Not automatable, and the criteria the spec assigns to a person:

- **Step 1** — text appears in the input line of the session the chord was pressed in and the pane focused when the daemon handled the start; the buffer shows it unsubmitted; the hostile canned transcript arrives as a single line with single spaces.
- **Step 2 (a)** — keypress to recording under 150 ms warm, over 10 consecutive attempts (`Scripts/measure.sh`).
- **Step 2 (b)** — a 20-second Russian dictation containing at least two English technical terms produces text whose meaning the user judges correct; the record shows `recognised` non-empty.
- **Step 2 (c)** — stop to injection for a 60-second utterance under 2 s.
- **Step 2 (d)** — the TCC prompt appears once, naming dicta; a rebuild and relaunch do not prompt again.
- **Step 3** — add a deliberately wrong rule, run one dictation, and name the offending rule from `recognised`, `final` and `rules` alone, without re-running anything.
- The chords fire and land in the session they were pressed in, verified against **Claude Code's** input line specifically — not only a fish prompt (F5).
- Sleep/wake and an AirPods route change during an attempt each end it with a visible fault and no injection.
- Daemon lifecycle: survives logout/login; a crash never leaves an indicator claiming a recording that is not happening.

## Not in this plan

- **Step 4** — the external filter command, its configuration and its fallback to **replaced**. The `Filter` seam exists as a `NoFilter` pass-through; nothing invokes a subprocess. D9c defers the choice of engine until real recogniser output has been seen, and that is a decision for the user.
- **Step 5** — the audit giving every row of §7 either an automated test or a checklist entry. Rows belonging to components built here are tested as they are built; the sweep that proves none was missed is step 5's job.
